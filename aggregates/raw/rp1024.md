# RP-1024 Vol. II (NASA Reference Publication 1024, 1978) - aggregate extraction

Source: `reports/dod-hdbk-743a-nasa-rp-1024/ADA473152.pdf` (NASA RP-1024, "Anthropometric Source Book Vol. II: A Handbook of Anthropometric Data", Webb Associates). Output: `aggregates/raw/rp1024.csv` (source_id `rp1024-vol2`, quality `B`), survey table: `aggregates/raw/rp1024_surveys.csv`.

## Method

1. **Layout.** PDF pages 98-425 are the data tables (book pp. 94-...): strictly alternating pages, **even PDF pages 98, 100, ..., 424 = `*METRIC VALUES*`** (164 pages), odd pages 99-425 = `*ENGLISH VALUES*` (skipped; spot-checked pp. 99, 101, 103, 251, 349, 423, 425). PDF p. 426 is the unit-conversion table, p. 427 the back cover. Rows are grouped under numbered variable headings (variable number = the RP-1024 "computer number", alphabetical from ABDOMINAL DEPTH, SITTING to WRIST HEIGHT).
2. **OCR.** Each metric page was rendered at 200 dpi with pymupdf, rotated by -90 degrees, and OCR'd with RapidOCR (cached per page as JSON). Tokens were deskewed (per-page slope fit) and shifted by a per-page x offset measured from the printed column headers (1ST...99TH), then grouped into lines by y coordinate. Row fields are read from the token text: survey number + printed name, MEAN, STD DEV, COEF OF V (`nn.nn%`), N (`****` = more than 10,000, left blank), and percentile columns (assigned to columns by right-edge x position). Percentiles that are printed in parentheses (computed from mean/SD) are ignored. Glued tokens (name+mean, CV+N, several percentiles in one token) are split with the CV check as the arbiter.
3. **Variable identification.** Variable headings were matched (number and fuzzy name) to the variable list in the "Definition of Measurements" section (PDF pp. 33-76; 298 variables, all found in the data pages). A few printed names differ from the definition names (Elbow Breadth, Femoral Breadth, Foot Breadth, Finger Diameter III, Finger II-Crotch Length) and were matched through the Index of Dimensions.
4. **Units.** No unit is printed on the metric pages; by convention (checked against magnitudes and the English pages) lengths/circumferences/arcs/skinfolds are cm (x10 -> mm), Weight and Weight as Reported are kg, Age is years. Skinfold means are 0.1-3 (cm) and were converted to mm (noted in `notes`). Grip Strength has no determinable unit and was dropped (14 rows).
5. **Measure keys.** Mapped to `database/harmonized_measures.csv` only for identical landmark/posture (e.g. Stature 805, Weight 957, Acromial Height 23, Sitting Height 758, Abdominal Depth Sitting 6, Biiliocristale Breadth 130 -> bicristal_breadth, ...; see `measure_key` values in the CSV). Waist Circumference (931) is mapped to `waist_circumference` (omphalion) only for surveys whose reference list says the waist was taken at omphalion/umbilicus (S19-21, 23, 24, 30, 31, 33, 48-51, 53-57, 59, 62, 80-85); for all others it is `other:waist_circumference_non_omphalion` (natural/minimum/trouser-band waist). Everything else is `other:<snake_case>`; e.g. Hand Breadth Across Thumb, circumferences at other levels, Elbow Breadth, etc. are not mapped.
6. **Survey table.** Survey numbers 1-85 come from the "Anthropometric Surveys: A Reference List" (PDF pp. 10-30; only surveys 1-85 are in this copy: book pp. 24-25 (surveys 86-91) are **missing from the scanned PDF**, and no survey 22 exists in the list). Country/type/sex/year/N were transcribed from those descriptions. Surveys 86-91 appear in the data tables and their metadata (country, service, year, sex) was inferred from the printed table names only (see `notes` and `in_pdf_reference_list=no` in the survey table). Where the reference list does not state the sex of a military/civilian sample it is recorded as `both` with a note (never guessed as M). Sub-samples: S7 (=S8+S9+S10), S11 (>S12), S19 (=S20+S21+S23), S25 (>S26-S29), S37 (>S38), S48 (=S49+S50+S51), S53 (>S54-S57), S80 (>S81,S82); S30/S31/S33 and S5/S14 are companion surveys. Because totals and sub-samples are all present, do not pool populations that overlap.
7. **Population name.** `S<no> <printed short name>`; the short names are the abbreviations printed in the data tables, cleaned of OCR noise (the printed name for each survey was chosen by voting over all OCR readings, with manual correction of obvious OCR letter errors).

## Checks applied to every row

* CV check: printed COEF OF V must equal 100*SD/MEAN within about +-0.085 (rounding of two-decimal values). Rows that fail are dropped, except (a) a single-character OCR error in MEAN or SD was repaired only when exactly one one-edit candidate satisfies the CV within the strict rounding tolerance (and, for MEAN, lies within 0.6 SD of the printed 50th percentile) and (b) a glued name/year digit or junk digit before the CV was removed only when it made the CV check pass uniquely.
* Percentiles: values must be non-decreasing and plausible against mean+z*SD; implausible/out-of-order individual percentiles (OCR junk) were discarded (row kept, p5/p50/p95 left blank if lost). Mean must lie inside the 1st-99th (or 5th-95th) percentile range.
* Identity check: survey number must be in the survey table and the printed name must agree with that survey's printed name (fuzzy match on the modal OCR reading). Rows in a variable block must appear in increasing survey number (longest increasing subsequence kept). Where the number was unreadable or wrong the number was restored only when the printed name (or, for name-less lines, N) identifies a unique survey lying between the neighbouring rows.

## Known limitations

* OCR (not born-digital): quality `B`. The CV check catches most misread mean/SD digits but not errors that keep CV within tolerance, nor errors in N or in percentile digits that keep percentiles monotone and plausible. N is unchecked (digits can be lost); a few N values may be wrong.
* Rows whose CV was garbled or whose survey identity could not be recovered were dropped rather than guessed, so some survey x variable cells are missing (see counts above).
* Only p5, p50, p95 are output; parenthesised (computed) percentiles are ignored; percentile columns were located by x position and may be blank where OCR merged tokens.
* Units are not printed on the metric pages; conventions inferred (see Method 4). Skinfold unit (cm) is inferred from magnitude. Grip strength omitted.
* Surveys 86-91 metadata (Korea, Sweden, Iran, Australia) is inferred from table names because book pp. 24-25 are missing in the PDF; sex is `both` (not stated) except S88 (male per printed name). Survey numbers >91 do not occur; no Indian survey appears in the tables of this copy. The introduction says 61 populations, whereas the tables number up to S91 (S22 absent).
* Many surveys are sub-samples/totals of one another (see Method 6) - do not pool. Waist and some other levels differ by survey. Several sub-populations (e.g. "Latin American", "NATO") are multinational.
* Values in RP-1024 were themselves derived by the editors from raw data, published means/SDs, or interpolated percentiles (see each survey description); parenthesised percentiles are estimated from mean/SD.
